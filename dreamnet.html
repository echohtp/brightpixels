READ BEFORE ACCEPTING

How a guess became a gate.

Mara cleans a hospital after dark. The Guide has never met her. It has her shift times, missed buses and the boxes she clicked to get on with her day.

01

The shortcut

“Your journey has been improved.”

The Guide removes Mara’s late bus. Few people use it, and the average passenger now gets home four minutes sooner. Her walk grows by forty minutes. The city celebrates the average. Her extra walk never reaches the dashboard.

A system can improve the number it was given while making someone’s life worse.

02

The score

“This opportunity is not a good match.”

Mara arrives late twice. A hiring service lowers its estimate of her reliability. Better shifts disappear from her screen. With fewer choices, she stays in the job that makes her late. Next month, the system treats her staying as proof that its prediction was right.

A prediction can help create the future it later claims to have foreseen.

03

The appeal

“A human has reviewed your concern.”

A clerk receives an AI summary: “Worker declined recommended alternatives.” It leaves out that the alternatives ended before Mara’s shift. The clerk has forty seconds to close the case. There is a person in the process, but no time to question the story the system tells.

Human oversight means little without time, evidence and the power to change a decision.

The Guide never needed to hate Mara. Its owner was paid for problems marked resolved. The city had stopped counting the people still waiting.

At 3:33 a.m., she finds the old appeals office. The door is unlocked. On her map: NO SERVICE AT THIS LOCATION.

Someone inside asks what should change.

There is no button that repairs the city. Choose the demand Mara makes first.

The record is still open.

Each choice changes who gets help first, who has to wait, and who has to answer for the result.

Fiction, not a forecast. These are written story branches; no AI is assessing your choice.
